Salal (*Gaultheria shallon*) is one of the most versatile and valuable native plants in Pacific Northwest landscapes. This evergreen shrub offers year-round interest, wildlife value, and incredible resilience.

## Why Grow Salal?

### Wildlife Value
- **Berries for birds** - Thrushes, waxwings, and other songbirds love the purple-black berries
- **Pollinator support** - Pink urn-shaped flowers attract native bees and hummingbirds
- **Cover habitat** - Dense growth provides shelter for ground-nesting birds

### Landscape Benefits
- **Erosion control** - Extensive root systems stabilize slopes
- **Groundcover** - Spreads to fill in under trees
- **Evergreen beauty** - Glossy leaves look good year-round
- **Drought tolerant** - Once established, needs no summer water

## Growing Conditions

Salal is incredibly adaptable:

| Condition | Preference |
|-----------|------------|
| Sun | Part shade to full shade (tolerates sun with moisture) |
| Soil | Acidic, well-drained (tolerates poor soils) |
| Moisture | Medium to dry |
| Zones | 6-9 |

## Care Tips

1. **Plant in fall** for best establishment
2. **Mulch well** with acidic materials like bark or needles
3. **Water first summer** then let nature take over
4. **Prune to control spread** if needed

## Companion Plants

Salal pairs beautifully with:
- Sword fern (*Polystichum munitum*)
- Oregon grape (*Mahonia aquifolium*)
- Red huckleberry (*Vaccinium parvifolium*)
- Western red cedar (*Thuja plicata*)

Add salal to your landscape for a truly Pacific Northwest feel!
