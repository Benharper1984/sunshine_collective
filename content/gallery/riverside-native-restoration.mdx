This project involved restoring 2 acres of degraded riparian habitat along a small urban creek. Years of invasive species had displaced native vegetation and degraded water quality.

## Challenges

- **Himalayan blackberry** had completely taken over the banks
- **Erosion** was causing sedimentation in the creek
- **Limited access** for heavy equipment
- **Sensitive timeline** to avoid nesting season impacts

## Our Approach

### Phase 1: Invasive Removal
- Manual removal of blackberry (no herbicides)
- Chipped material used as mulch on-site
- Root barriers installed in problem areas

### Phase 2: Restoration Planting
We installed over 500 native plants including:
- Red osier dogwood (*Cornus sericea*)
- Pacific willow (*Salix lasiandra*)
- Sword fern (*Polystichum munitum*)
- Salmonberry (*Rubus spectabilis*)
- Native sedges and rushes

### Phase 3: Bioengineering
- Live willow stakes for bank stabilization
- Coir logs to reduce erosion
- Rock weirs to slow water flow

## Outcomes

Two years after installation:
- **Native vegetation cover** increased from 5% to 75%
- **Bird species observed** doubled
- **Salmon spotted** in the creek for the first time in 10 years
- **Water quality** significantly improved

This project demonstrates how even heavily degraded sites can be restored with patience and proper techniques.
